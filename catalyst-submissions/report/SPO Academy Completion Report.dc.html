Delivered by Fluid7 Ltd with Edify27 October 2026
The problem this project addressed

Stake pool operators are, as a population, technically excellent and commercially invisible. Most can build and run infrastructure. Far fewer can say what their pool is for, who it serves, what value it creates, or how it survives.

SPO Academy was funded to close that gap with a business course written for operators, delivered on-chain, with a credential at the end.

Who uses it. Cardano stake pool operators, mainnet and testnet. The cohort included established operators, one running five years, and prospective operators without a mainnet pool yet, which the course was deliberately built to serve.

Key actions completed by users. Minting an Andamio access token, working the module assignments against their own pool, building a plan in the Playbook Builder, claiming an on-chain credential, and submitting written feedback.

Evidence of engagement. Each session has a published write-up and recording. Participants submitted assignments between sessions, raised platform friction in the cohort channel as it happened, and two have described continuing to build their plan after the cohort closed. Completion ran ahead of claiming: operators did the work and left the credential unclaimed, which is the same signal as their strongest product ask. A credential is worth claiming when there is somewhere to show it.

Impact

03
What changed for the operators

Every form respondent reported an unprompted shift in how they think about their pool, and all three said the plan they built is one they will use. One had already started an evolving playbook for their own pool. One stated they will execute on the plan.

SPO Academy · Project Completion Report06
03 · Impact

Asked in the final session what the course changed for them, the answers were:

  1. →seeing the gaps in how a pool measures the value it creates;
  2. →the difference between a pool's philosophy and the systems that run it;
  3. →thinking of a pool as an organism rather than a machine;
  4. →the pool's financial gaps and the types of delegator it serves;
  5. →and, for a five-year operator, putting words to the personal development the work has brought.
Before and after

Before this project there was no business-focused course for Cardano stake pool operators, and no on-chain credential for one. There is now, with nine operators through it and the material permanently available and self-paced.

Benefit to the Cardano ecosystem

Small pools that can articulate their purpose and sustain themselves are what keeps the network decentralised. The course also put Andamio's credentialing through a full live cohort and fed detailed findings back to their team, who asked for them and acted on them. Every course built on that platform benefits from that round trip.

Partnerships and recognition

Delivered with Edify. The course was presented in person to the xSPO Alliance weekly call on 4 October 2026, and announced across the Andamio, SPO Call, xSPO Alliance, RealFi, IOG / Intersect and Midnight communities.

SPO Academy · Project Completion Report09
On the participant count
9/ 15

Nine against a target of fifteen is the one criterion missed. Marketing was not the constraint.

Fourteen operators gave their contact details, ten through the persona quiz alone, across fifty-six published campaign items. The constraint was conversion from interest to commitment. A stake pool operator is usually a sole trader running infrastructure in the evenings, and a four-session course asking for written assignments about their own business is a real ask.

The nine who committed produced more usable evidence than fifteen passive enrolments would have.

SPO Academy · Project Completion Report10

Lessons learned

  1. 1
    A cohort target is a conversion assumption, not a marketing one.We reached operators comfortably and converted fewer than planned. The next target should be set on how many operators will commit to assignments, not on how many will give an email address.
  2. 2
    Choosing a platform is choosing a partner.Andamio gave us on-chain credentials we could not have built in the budget, and a team that answered. Participants still hit friction: submission costs the platform did not surface, a module stuck in assessment, no resume between modules. Andamio asked for the detail and shipped fixes while the cohort was still running. The rest are on their roadmap. Friction is inevitable. A team that responds is not.
  3. 3
    A self-paced course with a live series runs on two clocks.The course is deliberately asynchronous: the material stands alone and participants set their own pace, which is right for operators fitting study around running infrastructure. The four live sessions ran to a fixed calendar, which implied a pace the course does not impose. One participant worked four modules in the final week to meet a deadline that was never really there. Signpost which modules a session draws on, and let the operator decide whether to be ahead of it.
  4. 4
    Written feedback under-reports.Three of nine responded in writing. The verbal round in the final session surfaced four themes the form missed entirely, including the strongest product ask of the project, a credentials package operators can show to funders.
  5. 5
    Material written to teach a framework grows past what the work needs.Two respondents independently said the modules carry more words than the assignments require, and one said it read as machine-written. Length is not rigour.
  6. 6
    A free launch cohort gives no price signal.Nothing was charged, so the published prices remain untested.
SPO Academy · Project Completion Report11

Recommendations for future development

Before a second cohort:
  1. 1Build one complete worked SPO business model, end to end, and make it the spine of the course rather than an appendix. This was the clearest single ask.
  2. 2Cut the overlapping frameworks. Teach one model per idea and cross-reference the rest.
  3. 3Signpost rather than schedule. Say which modules each live session draws on, so a participant can choose where to be without the course becoming a timetable.
  4. 4Restructure the live hour as application, not re-presentation, and give it a written participation route for operators who would rather not speak.
  5. 5Clear the production debt: audio quality, narration pace, contrast in module 503, image sizing in module 100, and hyperlinked cross-references.
Beyond that:
  1. 6Build the credentials package. The cohort asked for a logbook they can put in front of partners and funders. A per-module credential nobody outside the platform can read is worth less than the same credential in a document an operator can send.
  2. 7Charge the second cohort, to test the pricing adopted in October.
  3. 8Work the platform feedback into Andamio's roadmap in a dedicated session rather than as individual tickets.

SPO Academy · Project Completion Report12
Final thoughts

The course works.

Operators who finished it think differently about their pools and have a plan they say they will use, which is the outcome the proposal was funded for. What this cohort proved is that the hard part is not teaching operators business, it is getting them to commit the hours.

That is a product problem, and it has answers: a worked example instead of theory, a pace someone can follow, and a credential worth showing to someone else.
